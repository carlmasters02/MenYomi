The person behind メニヨミ.

Carl Masters
Cybersecurity student · Solo builder · Tokyo

I'm a Cybersecurity student at Temple University Japan, based in Tokyo. I got into building things out of curiosity and quickly found I genuinely love the process, and MenYomi is one of the projects that came out of that.

MenYomi was born at the Agent Forge AI Hackathon in Tokyo, a one-day event built around a simple premise: the chatbot era is over, and the next generation of AI applications needs to access live data, run their own code, and ship to the real world. I built MenYomi solo, against teams of up to six, and pushed through a full day of technical hurdles to ship a working product by the deadline.

The idea is personal to the setting. Living in Tokyo, I watched how often a language barrier turns something as simple as ordering food into a stressful guess: handwritten menus, unfamiliar dishes, no idea what contains what. MenYomi is my answer. Point your camera at any Japanese menu and get it back translated, explained, and safe to order from.

Under the hood, it's not one model doing everything. It's six specialist systems working together. A self-hosted OCR model on Nosana reads the menu, with Qwen as a vision fallback; GMI translates and enriches each dish; ai& adds natural Japanese descriptions on Japan-based infrastructure; Daytona runs generated code safely in a sandbox; and the whole thing was built in Qoder. Wiring those six platforms into one clean experience as a solo developer was the real challenge, and the part I'm most proud of.

The goal was never just to translate words. It was to take something intimidating and make it feel effortless, so anyone, anywhere, can sit down at a Japanese restaurant and order with confidence.
